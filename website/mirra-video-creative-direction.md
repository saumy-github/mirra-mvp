# Mirra Website Video Creative Direction

Research and production brief · 20 August 2026

Evidence labels used throughout:

- **Observed** — directly visible in the Mirra codebase or a cited public source.
- **Interpretation** — a reasoned conclusion from the evidence.
- **Recommendation** — an original proposal for Mirra, not a claim about an existing campaign.

## 1. Executive Creative Summary

Mirra should not replace its placeholders with a collection of unrelated “AI fashion” clips. It should build one recognisable motion language: **Material Truth**.

Material Truth pairs the physical evidence shoppers trust—fabric weight, drape, seams, scale, body movement and garment construction—with the interface evidence merchants need—an in-page Try On control, an accurate avatar, size confidence, a retained storefront and an uninterrupted path to cart. The visual signature is a continuous hand-off from material to data and back again. A fold in fabric becomes a measurement contour; the contour becomes a Mirra interface line; the interface resolves into the same garment on a body.

The strongest launch system is:

1. Produce one 1–2 day motion-control/live-action master shoot covering a person, three garments, macro fabric, product-page interactions and packing-table actions.
2. Capture the real Mirra product separately at high resolution with deterministic demo data.
3. Add restrained 2D interface choreography and selective cloth simulation in post.
4. Derive 27 silent-first website loops across 11 placements from that shared material.
5. Reserve narrative sound for one optional hero film opened by the visitor; all inline loops remain muted.

The recommended hero is **The Continuous Fitting Room**: one apparently unbroken move from garment rail to fabric macro to Shopify PDP to personal avatar to one confident parcel. It expresses the entire value proposition without resorting to generic “AI magic.”

The work should make a merchant remember one idea: **Mirra turns material truth into purchase confidence without taking the shopper out of the store.**

## 2. Mirra Brand & Website Analysis

### What Mirra does

**Observed:** The current site positions Mirra as realistic virtual try-on for Shopify stores. Its copy stresses an on-product-page experience, existing catalogue compatibility, realistic garment representation, no app or marketplace redirect, brand ownership, a seven-day rollout and graceful storefront resilience.

**Interpretation:** Mirra is not primarily selling entertainment or avatar novelty. It is a B2B conversion and returns product whose consumer-facing experience must still feel desirable enough to use.

### Core value proposition

Mirra helps a fashion shopper make a better fit decision before purchase, inside the retailer’s existing product page. For the merchant, that means a credible route to fewer bracket orders, fewer avoidable returns, protected margin and a brand-owned customer relationship.

This commercial problem is material. The [2024 NRF/Happy Returns report](https://cdn.nrf.com/sites/default/files/2024-12/2024-Consumer-Returns-in%20the-Retail-Industry-Report_12.5.24.pdf) reports that 51% of surveyed Gen Z consumers had bracketed apparel or footwear purchases and that retailers saw bracketing in an average quarter of transactions. [Shopify’s current returns guidance](https://www.shopify.com/enterprise/blog/ecommerce-returns) likewise identifies fit and sizing as especially difficult to judge on apparel product pages. These figures establish the problem; they are not Mirra performance claims.

### Primary audience

**Observed:** The site speaks directly to Shopify fashion businesses, store operators and retail economics.

**Interpretation:** The primary decision audience is fashion founders, e-commerce directors, digital-product leaders, merchandising leaders and operations/returns stakeholders. The secondary audience is the shopper whose experience a merchant is evaluating.

Geography is not specified in the repository. The product language and Shopify positioning suggest global relevance; India is a plausible early market but should not be asserted in production until confirmed.

There is one positioning inconsistency to resolve before final production: the document-level metadata describes a consumer-facing personal 3D avatar, while the marketing pages sell merchant infrastructure. Hero film, page metadata and sales narrative should agree on the B2B buyer and the shopper experience they are buying.

### Desired emotional impression

Mirra wants to feel:

- exact, trustworthy and materially literate;
- embedded and operationally low-risk;
- fashion-aware without becoming a fashion editorial that obscures the product;
- technically advanced without sci-fi visual clichés;
- optimistic about commerce without making unsupported performance promises.

### Existing visual identity

**Observed:** The marketing site uses a high-contrast editorial sans-serif system, large typographic fields, black and warm/light grounds, acid lime, mint and blue section accents, draggable horizontal rails and oversized fashion/product-media panels. Motion is abundant and the current clips are MWG portfolio placeholders.

**Interpretation:** The brand already has confident color and scale. Replacement film should calm and focus that energy, not add more unrelated spectacle. The bright colors work best as spatial fields, UI selections or controlled light—not as rainbow grading applied to skin and garments.

### Major website journey

1. **Hero — recognise the proposition.** Understand “realistic virtual try-on for Shopify” in seconds.
2. **Why Mirra — understand the problem.** Fit uncertainty becomes hesitation, bracketing, returns and margin loss.
3. **Outcomes — believe the commercial consequence.** See earlier fit decisions, fewer backup sizes and retained margin.
4. **Product features — understand the mechanism.** One button, existing catalogue, realistic result, in-browser journey, brand ownership and fast rollout.
5. **Mirra in action — inspect proof.** See real use cases rather than mood film.
6. **Commerce principles and CTA — assess fit and act.** Confirm compatibility and begin a pilot/demo conversation.

### What the current placeholder videos are trying to do

The hero reel supplies kinetic spectacle and a drag interaction. The three outcome panels use cinematic background motion to make commercial claims feel substantial. The six feature cards are meant to demonstrate individual mechanisms. The five “Mirra in action” clips should be proof but currently read as another abstract reel. Replacement media must sharpen these roles rather than merely improve production value.

### Known production inputs and uncertainties

- Mirra URL: local application/repository; no public production URL was supplied.
- Brand guidelines: inferred from the live code and supplied references.
- Assets: current product UI, a limited fashion-image set and placeholder motion; availability of customers, licensed models, garments, locations, brand partners, source product footage and production-ready 3D garments is unknown.
- Budget: not defined. This report provides lean, moderate and premium routes.
- Claims/legal review: required before any merchant name, client result, speed or returns metric appears on screen.

## 4. Research Methodology

Research was conducted on 20 August 2026 in four layers:

1. **First-party Mirra audit:** route, component, copy, media-source and playback-behaviour review of the local marketing implementation.
2. **Category research:** official product sites and product documents from Google Shopping, True Fit, Veesual, 3DLOOK, Style.me, Sizebay, Fit Analytics and Reactive Reality.
3. **Creative benchmark research:** original brand/studio work from FutureDeluxe, Tendril, ManvsMachine, Apple and H&M-linked creators, plus Awwwards, Vimeo, Behance and fashion-film award archives.
4. **Platform and UX research:** official TikTok business material, Meta/Instagram research, Pinterest business guidance, W3C, MDN and web.dev.

Priority was given to original brand, studio and standards sources. Social-platform constraints are material:

- Public TikTok pages often block automated access; conclusions use TikTok’s own Fashion Playbook and public business case studies.
- Instagram posts are inconsistently accessible without authentication; conclusions use official Meta trend reports and accessible original brand/studio sources.
- Pinterest contains many unattributed reposts. Pins are used only for pattern observation when the original creator could not be verified, never as proof of authorship.
- Vimeo and Behance references were selected where credits and dates were visible.

No engagement statistic, creator, date or campaign name has been invented. Unknown production inputs remain flagged rather than filled with assumptions.

## 6. Social / Platform Research Findings

### TikTok

The official [TikTok Fashion Playbook](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/ENG_TikTok_Fashion_META_Playbook_New.pdf) recommends a strong first 3–5 seconds and recognises try-on hauls, style variety, creator point of view, lists and expectation-versus-reality structures. TikTok’s [XSide Try-On case study](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/inspiration/XSide-try-on-revolution?redirected=1) also shows the platform actively productising AI try-on for creator advertising.

**Implication:** Mirra should borrow immediate visual causality and honest comparison, not TikTok editing conventions wholesale. A website loop cannot depend on voiceover, comments, trending audio or a punchline at second 18.

### Instagram / Reels

Meta’s [2025 India review](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/12/what-kept-india-scrolling-in-2025-instagrams-year-in-review-is-here/) notes nostalgia, lo-fi digicam texture and unusual ultra-wide reel formats; Meta’s [Reels research in India](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/09/five-years-on-reels-reigns-as-indias-top-short-form-video-platform/) reinforces a video-first discovery environment.

**Implication:** Small doses of documentary texture can make Mirra feel human, but ultra-thin frames, meme pacing and lo-fi overlays are short-lived social devices. They should not become the website’s core language.

### Pinterest

Pinterest’s [2026 marketing playbook](https://business.pinterest.com/en-gb/pdf/pinterest-predicts/2026-marketing-playbook) highlights max-width video, branded collage and shoppable collection formats. Public pins also reveal sustained appetite for macro textile loops, although authorship is often unclear.

**Implication:** Build poster-worthy, saveable frames and garment/material taxonomies. Treat unattributed pins as mood signals only.

### Vimeo, Behance and motion portfolios

Credited Vimeo fashion films reward authored cinematography and performance; Behance and studio portfolios show sophisticated cloth simulation, mixed media and product-led metaphors. The strongest examples do not add effects indiscriminately: they choose one physical idea and push it consistently.

**Implication:** Mirra’s “how did they make that?” moment should come from a seamless material-to-interface transition, not from random impossible imagery.

## 9. Trends Mirra Should Avoid

1. **AI shimmer transformations.** Overused, poorly connected to fit accuracy and likely to date quickly.
2. **Uncanny synthetic people.** One malformed hand or sliding garment destroys the trust Mirra needs.
3. **Liquid chrome as default “premium.”** Saturated across beauty and tech; it says nothing about fit.
4. **Neon cyber grids, scanning bodies and biometric HUDs.** They create surveillance anxiety and misrepresent a friendly commerce tool.
5. **Influencer haul language on the website.** Useful on social; noisy, trend-dependent and weak as evergreen B2B proof.
6. **Floating-device parades.** They abstract the experience away from the real Shopify product page.
7. **Speed-ramped montage without causality.** Fast is not the same as clear.
8. **Fake client dashboards or outcome counters.** Never visualise an unverified conversion or returns result.
9. **Bodies as interchangeable “skins.”** Representation must retain personhood and avoid objectification.
10. **Scroll-jacked full-screen film.** It can impair navigation, accessibility and low-power mobile performance.
11. **Sound-led comprehension.** Inline website video must communicate when muted.
12. **One 16:9 master cropped everywhere.** It will amputate garment and interface evidence on mobile.

## 10. Recommended Mirra Video Creative System

### Creative-direction name: Material Truth

**Creative thesis:** A trustworthy digital fitting room begins with respect for the physical garment. Mirra’s motion system makes the passage from cloth to data—and from data to confident purchase—visible, elegant and understandable.

### Cinematography

- Motion-control dolly and overhead rigs for repeatable match cuts.
- 50–85mm normal/portrait lenses for people and garment silhouette; 90–120mm macro for fibre, seam and fastener detail.
- Static or extremely slow moves for UI proof; no gratuitous handheld during product explanation.
- Hands and posture before faces. Cast faces when emotion or representation matters, not as generic lifestyle decoration.
- Frame the full garment at least once in every product-led clip.

### Lighting and color

- Soft directional daylight/studio light that reveals texture; controlled hard edge for silhouettes.
- Neutral skin and garment color. Do not grade brand garments into Mirra’s palette.
- Use the site’s lime, mint and blue as background cards, interface selection states or physical gel reflections in no more than one plane per shot.
- Blacks retain detail; whites remain warm rather than clinical.

### Environment, props and wardrobe

- One adaptable studio: warm neutral cove, garment rail, clean packing table and a product-photography surface.
- Props must advance the story: size tabs, two vs one parcel, measuring tape used sparingly, pattern paper, garment labels, laptop/desktop and phone.
- Wardrobe spans a structured layer, fluid dress/top and stretch/knit piece to prove different behaviours.
- Avoid trend-heavy styling that shortens the footage’s useful life.

### Motion graphics and UI

- Capture the actual product against locked demo data.
- Use simple crop reveals, mask transitions, measurement contours and one scanning/fit line where justified.
- Typography stays part of the page whenever possible; do not bake essential copy into video.
- UI animation uses physical continuity: a seam direction can set the axis of a panel transition.

### Editing rhythm and texture

- Loops breathe: one readable action, one consequence, one return.
- Hero master: 2–3 second beats; inline loops: 1.5–2.5 second beats.
- Match on motion, geometry or color—not generic whip pans.
- Add very subtle grain only to unify live-action and CG; keep product UI pixel-clean.

### 3D philosophy

Use 3D where it reveals something a camera cannot: cloth construction, pattern-to-drape, layer separation or a continuous scale transition. Do not use 3D merely to make logos float.

### Sound philosophy

Inline autoplay is always muted. The optional hero film can use restrained tactile sound—rail click, fabric sweep, soft UI confirmation, parcel fold—and a minimal tonal score. Never rely on speech for comprehension; caption any dialogue.

### Loop philosophy

Build reversible physical actions or match the last composition to the first. Hide the seam in a fabric occlusion, browser-panel wipe, camera pass behind a garment or identical start/end hand position. Avoid obvious jump cuts every five seconds.

### Eight system rules

1. Every clip proves **body, garment or store**—preferably two.
2. Physical material leads; technology responds.
3. One transformation mechanism per clip.
4. The real interface remains legible and functionally accurate.
5. First frame works as a poster and a proposition.
6. Every master is intentionally reframed for desktop and mobile.
7. Silent-first, pauseable, reduced-motion-safe and performance-budgeted.
8. Never imply a customer, result or garment behaviour that Mirra cannot verify.

- **Original execution / production:** Product capture + macro pickup shoot; Medium / Moderate.
- **Risk / fit:** Requires five robust demo garments and no fake client attribution.

#### V11-B — One Look, Built in Layers

- **One-line idea:** Across five rail cards, a complete outfit builds layer by layer while each card still works as an independent loop.
- **Purpose:** Demonstrate styling, saved looks and product discovery.
- **Core visual / opening:** The same avatar neutral in card one; each subsequent card introduces a garment category.
- **Sequence / ending:** Top → bottom → outer layer → shoe/accessory if supported → saved signature look; each local clip adds/removes its layer.
- **Camera / edit:** Locked avatar stage, category-level match cuts.
- **UI / human:** Actual layer controls and saved-look flow; avatar only.
- **Graphics / color / sound:** Card number/category; garment-true colors; muted.
- **Duration:** 6 seconds each.
- **References:** [Style.me styling](https://style.me/virtual-fitting/), [Veesual mix-and-match](https://www.veesual.ai/solutions/e-commerce).
- **Original execution / production:** Real product capture + light motion; Low–Medium / Lean–Moderate.
- **Risk / fit:** Only include supported categories and real layer ordering.

#### V11-C — Proof Cards

- **One-line idea:** Five cards each pair a verifiable merchant problem with the exact Mirra interaction that addresses it.
- **Purpose:** Create a B2B proof rail for buyers who need mechanism more than fashion mood.
- **Core visual / opening:** Plain claim label such as “Size hesitation” over its corresponding product-page state.
- **Sequence / ending:** Problem state → interaction → observable product outcome → neutral poster; no business KPI unless measured.
- **Camera / edit:** Screen-led, documentary merchant hands and direct cuts.
- **UI / human:** Real software and optional anonymous merchant operator.
- **Graphics / color / sound:** Page typography; optional sound only in expanded case film.
- **Duration:** 7–9 seconds.
- **References:** [True Fit](https://www.truefit.com/get-started), [Google Shopping VTO](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/shopping/studio-quality-digital-try-on/), [Shopify returns guidance](https://www.shopify.com/enterprise/blog/ecommerce-returns).
- **Original execution / production:** Product capture + one interview/pickup day; Medium / Moderate.
- **Risk / fit:** Problem labels must not be presented as measured Mirra outcomes.

## 12. Detailed Hero Video Treatments

### Hero treatment 1 — The Continuous Fitting Room

**Creative thesis:** Mirra is not a destination between shopping and buying; it is the continuous material layer that lets a garment carry its truth through the digital decision.

#### 18-second treatment and storyboard

| Time | Shot and subject movement | Camera / transition | UI / typography |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0–2.0s | Structured jacket on rail. Hand brushes the sleeve; it swings forward. | Slow motion-control push, 65mm. Sleeve occludes lens. | No baked copy. Page headline remains outside video. |
| 2.0–4.0s | The sleeve’s weave fills frame; light traces a seam. | Macro probe. Fibre geometry subtly straightens into pixels. | One seam line becomes a product-image edge. |
| 4.0–6.5s | Mirra-enabled PDP appears at real scale. Cursor selects Try On. | Virtual camera eases from macro to orthographic screen. | Real retailer header, product, size and Try On control. |
| 6.5–9.0s | Avatar resolves from the shopper’s saved body profile; jacket layer settles. | Camera arcs 20° around the stage. Transition is a real panel open, not a glow. | One brief “Your profile” state; no fake measurement readout. |
| 9.0–11.5s | Shopper turns shoulder; avatar view changes on the same beat. | Match-cut physical and avatar posture. | View selector remains legible. |
| 11.5–13.5s | One size is selected and added to cart. | Camera follows button movement into a white field. | Only authentic confirmation. |
| 13.5–16.0s | White field becomes tissue paper around one parcel; hands close it. | Overhead motion-control. | Garment/size label only. |
| 16.0–18.0s | Tissue lifts and fills frame, becoming the jacket sleeve at 0.0. | Hidden loop stitch in moving fabric. | None. |

**Lighting reference:** Soft north-window key plus a narrow raking source for weave; warm-neutral cove and deep but detailed charcoal.

**Palette:** Warm white #F4F1EA, near-black #1B1D1C, material-accurate garment colors and one site-lime confirmation.

**Sound design:** A rail click, fibre sweep, low interface confirmation, tissue fold and a restrained sub-bass tonal bed. No voiceover. Inline cut is silent.

**Desktop composition:** 16:9 or 2:1 safe master with action concentrated in the central 70%; enough quiet field for the page headline.

**Mobile adaptation:** Shoot dedicated 4:5/9:16 passes. Keep hand, garment, avatar and CTA in a vertical centre line; split the wide packing-table shot into a close overhead.

**Production requirements:** Motion-control dolly, macro lens/probe, repeatable rail rig, overhead rig, one cast shopper, hand model/wardrobe stylist, garment hero, neutral studio, product screen captures, previs, clean plates, compositor, 2D motion designer and cloth/VFX artist.

**AI/CG opportunities:** CG only for macro-scale continuity and verified cloth layer settling. AI can assist roto/cleanup or previs; do not generate the garment/body result used as product proof.

**Production shortcut:** Replace the continuous camera with four visibly intentional match cuts using fabric occlusions; use one garment and one hand/body setup.

**Lean alternative:** Screen-led PDP journey plus two macro stock/custom pickups and a tabletop parcel shot; 8–10 seconds.

**Premium alternative:** Fully previsualised 20-second motion-control sequence with product-accurate digital garment and responsive light bridge.

### Hero treatment 2 — One Garment, Three Truths

**Creative thesis:** The most persuasive claim of realism is a fair, simultaneous comparison.

#### 15-second treatment and storyboard

| Time | Shot | Camera / movement | Transition and UI |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0–1.5s | One seam aligns across garment, model and avatar panels. | Three locked, calibrated frames. | Panels draw on from the seam. |
| 1.5–4.0s | Hand lifts physical hem; model lifts heel; avatar rotates slightly. | Motion-control/turntable sync. | No labels yet—let the relation read. |
| 4.0–6.5s | Model performs a quarter turn; folds form in all panels. | 65mm full-body, identical timing. | Fine registration ticks appear briefly. |
| 6.5–9.0s | Mirra size/view selection changes. | Right panel expands, then returns to triptych. | Actual control and state. |
| 9.0–12.0s | Macro cuff, shoulder and hem details appear in three stacked beats. | Matched crop and focus. | Verified labels only: cuff / shoulder / hem. |
| 12.0–15.0s | All settle to neutral; seams align to opening. | Slow final drift, then hold poster frame. | Mirra mark may appear outside video, never over evidence. |

**Lighting/palette:** Identical neutral lighting and calibrated color across physical/model capture; warm-white dividers and charcoal UI.

**Sound:** Three tactile stems—hanger, footfall, UI—resolve into one tone; silent inline.

**Desktop/mobile:** Desktop is horizontal triptych. Mobile becomes a vertical sequence with a brief stacked comparison, not three unreadably narrow columns.

**Requirements:** Product hero, model, garment stylist, calibrated turntable/motion control, actual avatar and product build, edit/composite supervisor.

**AI/CG:** Cleanup and alignment only. Product output must come from Mirra.

**Lean alternative:** Physical garment macro + actual avatar in a two-panel comparison; omit model shoot.

**Premium alternative:** Three garments/materials and repeatable movement library, creating all V07/V11 derivatives.

### Hero treatment 3 — Drape as Data

**Creative thesis:** Measurements are not cold numbers; they are the quiet structure that lets digital cloth respect a person and a garment.

#### 16-second treatment and storyboard

| Time | Shot | Camera / subject | Transition / UI |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0–2.5s | Light crosses a satin fold at macro scale. | 100mm macro slider, 60fps. | Fold edge gains a fine graphite contour. |
| 2.5–5.0s | Contour becomes an overhead pattern line; two verified dimensions appear. | Virtual scale pull to rostrum. | Labels use actual demo values and fade quickly. |
| 5.0–7.5s | Pattern panels lift and wrap a neutral digital form. | Product-accurate cloth simulation. | No “AI sparkle”; seams meet mechanically. |
| 7.5–10.5s | Form resolves into the Mirra avatar wearing the garment. | Slow 20° orbit, full-body reveal. | Actual Mirra stage/control edge visible. |
| 10.5–13.0s | Physical wearer moves once; digital drape responds in matched cut. | Locked live-action and screen pairing. | None. |
| 13.0–16.0s | Camera pushes into the garment’s fold and arrives at opening frame. | Macro/CG surface hand-off. | Hold a clean poster at 15.5s. |

**Lighting/palette:** Pearlescent warm whites, charcoal contours, garment-accurate material color and small mint/lime status cues.

**Sound:** Close fabric friction, pencil/pattern-paper trace and quiet interface confirmation.

**Desktop/mobile:** Desktop allows broad negative space around the macro surface. Mobile follows a single centre fold and full-body silhouette; labels remain outside the video when possible.

**Requirements:** Macro DP, textile stylist, pattern maker/consultant, 3D cloth artist, actual product capture and compositing.

**AI/CG:** Houdini/CLO-style cloth tools for deterministic simulation. AI may generate previs texture ideas but not final product evidence.

**Lean alternative:** Shoot real textile macros and use 2D contour animation into existing avatar capture.

**Premium alternative:** Scan/capture the physical hero garment, simulate from verified material parameters and build a seamless full-CG macro journey.

## 14. Production Feasibility Analysis

### What can share one production

One master studio setup can produce most of V01–V11:

- **Garment rail / neutral cove:** V01 hero, V06 catalogue, V07 realism and V11 category cards.
- **Calibrated full-body turntable:** V01-B, V03-B, V07 and V11.
- **Macro textile table:** V01-A/C, V02-B, V07 and transition plates across the site.
- **Packing table / overhead rig:** V03, V04 and V10.
- **Phone/desktop product setup:** V02, V05, V08 and V09.
- **Merchant/admin pickup session:** V06, V10 and V11-C.

### Production method by asset family

| Family | Recommended method | Key dependency | Feasibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| UI proof | Deterministic screen capture, motion design, optional screen replacement | Stable production/demo build and approved data | High |
| Garment macro | Live action with macro lens and textile stylist | Three visually distinct, camera-ready garments | High |
| Body/garment comparison | Controlled live action + actual Mirra result | A garment/avatar combination the engine renders reliably | Medium |
| Packing/logistics | Practical tabletop and motion control | Props, labels and claims-safe art direction | High |
| Continuous hero | Previs, live action, virtual camera, composite and limited cloth CG | Experienced VFX supervisor and locked UI | Medium |
| Product-accurate cloth CG | Pattern/material source, cloth artist and validation | Accurate garment inputs and product approval | Medium–low until assets are confirmed |

### Minimum team

For the moderate route: creative director/director, producer, DP, gaffer, motion-control or experienced camera grip, DIT, production designer/prop stylist, fashion stylist, hair/makeup as required, model/hand talent, product designer, UI capture operator, editor, compositor/motion designer, colorist, sound designer and accessibility/performance QA.

The premium route adds a VFX supervisor, previs artist, 3D cloth team, scan/material capture and dedicated mobile-unit framing. The lean route can be handled by a small tabletop crew plus product/motion team.

### Production risks and mitigations

1. **Product proof does not match generated beauty footage.** Lock hero garments only after a Mirra output test and product-lead approval.
2. **UI changes during post.** Capture against a tagged product build and keep interface inserts modular.
3. **Unapproved client implication.** Use “Mirra demo” and fictional/house storefront themes until written partner approval exists.
4. **Unrealistic cloth claims.** Separate artistic material transition from product-accuracy shots; label illustrative visualisation if necessary.
5. **Body representation feels extractive.** Cast collaboratively, show people as decision-makers and validate inclusive language/crops.
6. **Mobile crops destroy evidence.** Storyboard vertical in parallel and record dedicated passes.
7. **Large asset payload.** Set budgets before production; design shots that compress well—controlled grain, simple backgrounds and limited high-frequency particle noise.

## 15. Website UX / Performance Considerations

### Playback behaviour

- Inline video: autoplay only when muted and in/near viewport; loop; no sound track where possible.
- Expanded hero film: explicit Play action, visible controls, captions and user-controlled sound.
- Pause offscreen videos with IntersectionObserver. The current page can render 27 video elements; it should not decode all of them simultaneously.
- Provide one discoverable “Pause motion” control that governs every persistent loop and remembers the session preference.
- Under prefers-reduced-motion, render poster frames or one short crossfade—not merely paused frames that appear broken.

**Current implementation finding:** No explicit IntersectionObserver/page-visibility playback manager was found. Live review showed three hero clips still playing roughly 6,500–7,000 pixels after the hero had left the desktop viewport. The replacement project should treat playback control as an integration deliverable, not only an encoding task. Informative feature videos also currently carry accessible labels describing Mirra functionality while showing unrelated footage; replacement media and accessible naming must be reviewed together.

[MDN’s autoplay guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay) explains why muted/no-audio media is generally eligible for autoplay, but eligibility is not a reason to animate everything. [WCAG 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html) requires a mechanism to pause, stop or hide automatically moving content that lasts more than five seconds and is presented alongside other content unless essential.

### Performance budget

- Hero poster: AVIF/WebP, ideally below 150 KB, explicitly sized and eligible for LCP prioritisation.
- Initial hero video: no more than roughly 1.2–1.8 MB for the first desktop loop; mobile derivative 600 KB–1 MB. Treat these as engineering targets, then validate perceptual quality and real-device metrics.
- Secondary inline loop: target 350–800 KB per derivative; load only near viewport.
- Supply AV1 or VP9/WebM where browser support and CDN negotiation permit, with H.264 MP4 fallback.
- Remove audio streams from silent loops.
- Use multiple resolutions and media queries rather than serving the desktop master to mobile.
- Set width, height and aspect-ratio to prevent layout shifts.
- Use preload="none" or metadata for below-fold cards, poster images and delayed source attachment.
- Do not use particle noise, film grain or rapid full-frame cuts where a calmer shot tells the story; these compress poorly.

[web.dev’s video performance guide](https://web.dev/learn/performance/video-performance) and [lazy-loading guidance](https://web.dev/articles/lazy-loading-video) support poster/LCP planning and deferred video loading.

### Composition and accessibility

- Keep essential text in HTML, not baked into footage.
- Caption any spoken expanded film and supply a transcript.
- Maintain contrast for overlaid page copy across the entire loop; use a tested scrim if needed.
- Avoid flashing and high-frequency strobing.
- Write useful accessible names for explanatory video; decorative loops should be hidden from assistive technology.
- Poster frames must communicate the same essential fact as the loop.
- Touch rails need non-drag navigation and keyboard controls.
- Do not make understanding depend on hover, audio or precise scrubbing.

### Core Web Vitals validation

Test the final media build on representative low/mid-range Android hardware and Safari iOS, not only desktop broadband. Measure LCP, INP, memory and decoded-frame activity with autoplay on, motion paused and reduced-motion enabled. The homepage should remain usable if every video request fails.

## 18. Production Roadmap

### Phase 0 — Truth and rights audit (week 1)

- Confirm target markets, audience hierarchy, claims, seven-day promise and privacy language.
- Identify five product-safe demo garments and one hero garment.
- Secure model, music, garment, location and client/brand permissions.
- Freeze a deterministic Mirra demo environment and decide which output is product proof versus illustrative film.

### Phase 1 — Creative development and previs (weeks 2–3)

- Storyboard all 11 placements as one system.
- Build animatics in desktop and mobile aspect ratios.
- Compression-test representative fabric, skin, UI and grain shots.
- Run product-accuracy review on V07 and hero.
- Approve poster frames before shoot.

### Phase 2 — Product capture (week 3)

- Record PDP, profile, avatar, try-on, layers, size/view, cart and admin/activation states.
- Capture at a stable resolution with pointer/touch passes separated.
- Export clean UI plates for localisation and later product updates.

### Phase 3 — Master shoot (week 4)

- Day 1: garment rail, full-body/turntable, human decision moments and macro textiles.
- Day 2: phone/desktop, overhead catalogue, packing/returns and seven-day process.
- Premium optional day 3: motion-control hero transitions and material scan/reference.

### Phase 4 — Post-production (weeks 5–7)

- Edit the low-risk product clips first.
- Build V11 and V07 comparison proof.
- Composite hero only after those outputs pass product review.
- Color, sound, captions/transcript and poster extraction.
- Produce desktop and mobile derivatives; do not auto-crop.

### Phase 5 — Integration and validation (week 8)

- Lazy-load sources and pause offscreen media.
- Add motion pause and reduced-motion posters.
- Test browser matrix, mobile bandwidth, Core Web Vitals, contrast and keyboard/touch controls.
- Legal/product/brand sign-off for every visible label and claim.
- Run an A/B test on comprehension/engagement only after accessibility and performance gates pass.

## 19. Lean-Budget Version

The lean version can still feel authored if it is disciplined:

- Produce V05, V08, V09 and all five V11 clips primarily from real product capture.
- Shoot one half-day of macro garment material and one half-day overhead/hand/parcel footage.
- Build V02 and V03 from those practical assets.
- Use V01-B Three Truths in a two-panel simplification—physical macro plus real avatar—instead of the continuous VFX hero.
- Use 2D contour animation for V07; omit product-accurate cloth CG.
- Make V04 a graphic overhead loop and V10 a screen-led seven-frame sequence.
- License no stock people presented as customers. If no model budget exists, use hands, actual avatar views and garments.

Indicative production profile: small crew, one garment/studio location, one cast/hand talent, product capture and a strong editor/motion designer. The creative signature remains the same because the match cuts come from real seams, folds, panels and interface geometry.

## 20. Premium Version

The premium route makes Material Truth a defensible brand asset:

- Previsualise and shoot the full Continuous Fitting Room with motion control.
- Scan or accurately reconstruct one hero garment and capture material reference.
- Commission product-accurate cloth simulations for V01/V07.
- Cast a representative group and direct movement tests across garment categories.
- Build a calibrated turntable comparison library for ongoing product launches.
- Capture dedicated desktop, portrait and vertical plates rather than crops.
- Commission original tactile sound and a modular sonic signature.
- Produce a 45–60 second opt-in brand/product film from the same shoot, while keeping website loops concise.
- Create a reusable Mirra motion toolkit—transition templates, UI-capture rules, grade, poster system and aspect-ratio templates—for future customer stories.

The single concept with the greatest potential to become a recognisable Mirra signature is **the material-to-interface seam transition** inside The Continuous Fitting Room. It is ownable because it arises from Mirra’s subject—garments becoming truthful digital decisions—rather than from a generic visual trend.

### Decision summary

If only one production is approved, make **The Continuous Fitting Room** and deliberately capture its UI, macro, body, garment and packing components as modular plates. That single shoot can generate the hero, three outcomes, five feature demonstrations and the five-card proof rail while keeping the entire website in one visual world.
