## Five fidelity surfaces

**Typography.** The reference's high-contrast editorial serif is followed with a Times New Roman serif fallback for display headings. Controls and explanatory text use the existing system sans-serif stack. Display size is reduced on narrow screens; headings, labels and units remain readable without overlap. Navigation and form controls do not inherit the serif. Exact font matching is not claimed: the reference does not identify a licensed typeface.

**Spacing and layout.** A quiet full-page shell replaces the enclosing Profile card and sidebar as requested. Desktop uses QR plus pose guidance, review uses preview plus form, and mobile stacks these regions. The close control stays at the top. Thin form rules help associate values with labels. The extra reference pose columns were removed because the current model needs front and side body views plus a face capture.

**Colors and tokens.** Scoped `#FCFBF9` background, near-black `#201F1D` text and `#242321` actions, muted warm greys, and subdued green completion markers preserve the supplied palette. Existing application/Profile/Studio color tokens were not rethemed. No decorative gradients or bouncing/scale effects were added to the setup flow.

**Image quality.** Generated front, side and face examples share warm lighting, a neutral background and practical capture clothing. Front and side examples retain the whole body. The images are labelled examples, not account avatars. The build screen uses a deliberately blurred illustrative image while actual job state supplies its text. Production review uses the existing real GLB viewer and face attachment; the QA-only illustration cannot verify final body/face integration quality.

**Copy and content.** Product copy describes photos, fit and review rather than internal pipelines. The QR is real and expiring; copy/device actions replace a pretend SMS action. Local-only guidance explains why a localhost link cannot reach a different phone. The review shows supported available fields rather than just the two illustrative sliders. Native camera access still requires the phone user's tap/permission. Shopify prices, carts and installation success are not fabricated.
